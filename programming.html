First Projects

The first projects that I made were .bat terminal games and simple games on scratch. Sadly, since my .bat files died with my old laptop, and my scratch account is inaccessible due to my email getting hacked a few years ago, I won't be able to showcase them here.

My first big project was probably this game called "Rickroll Simulator."

While my friend and I were attending a summer camp together, he joked that I should turn rick-rolling (a recently popular trolling method) into a game. I took the suggestion seriously and made this: a game about checking your email and dodging rickrolls.

It features an email panel to the left with a giant "It's a RICKROLL" button if you suspect the email is trying to rickroll you. To the right, there are these "rickroll detectors," which get used up when you click on them and display the rickroll status of the current email in the terminal (in the bottom right). The terminal also featured some simple commands and debugging features for testing the game.

However, I didn't really know much about mobile-responsive websites at the time, so the entire website kind of breaks if you use any sort of different screen size.

After making this game, I created a few more websites, slowly learning more about HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

For example, this is a simple stopwatch that I made using .hta files.

And this is a chess website that I made with a chess JS library and a background that I made in Blender. The table was a free asset that I downloaded and the donuts were from the famous Donut Tutorial by Blender Guru. I just used a physics simulation to scatter them.

I also followed a few tutorials to make simple functional apps, like this ToDo list app.

Recent Projects

During my freshmen year of high school, I started taking CS50 courses. I thought that some of my final projects would be notable to share here (without the code, of course).

This was my final project for CS50x. It's a colliding box simulator, inspired by a video from 3Blue1Brown. The idea was that boxes with specific ratios would compute pi in the amount of collisions between the two boxes.

My final project for CS50P was a physics-based gravity game. The red circles repulse the player (the black circle) and the blue ones attract it. The goal of the game was to get the player to the green circle (the goal).

For my CS50 Web final project, I made a course builder. The owner of a course could add or delete elements from a course, and I even added a node system for custom logic.

More recently, I've made a few smaller projects.

This is a Mandlebrot set that I rendered with a custom Python script. It technecally supports generating images with Perturbation Theory, but my renderer isn't optimized enough for that to be remotely useful (it does make the output look weird though!).
